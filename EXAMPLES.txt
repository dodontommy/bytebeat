bytebeat -- starter expressions
==============================

YOU DO NOT HAVE TO TYPE ANY OF THESE.

  p        generate a patch into the focused layer -- always makes a sound
  P        mutate it (same shape, new numbers)
  C        undo
  n / b    step through the 14 patches below
  1-8      focus a layer;  shift+number  toggles it on/off
  V / v    sequencer on / edit the 16-step pattern
  M        record the selected knob as a motion loop
  Z        eight-track performance view and master phrase looper
  ?        help

The expressions below are what those keys are producing underneath, and are
worth reading once you want to steer rather than roll dice.

These are the same 14 patches compiled into the binary. In the instrument,
'n' and 'b' step forward and back through them; loading one sets the
expression, the output mode, all eight knobs AND the post chain, so every
one of them makes a sound immediately.

The knob values below are the shipped defaults. They are starting points,
not settings -- the whole reason p0..p7 are on single keys is that you are
supposed to be moving them.

Verified: every patch below produces signal between 19% and 65% of full
scale, measured over 40k samples with the DC blocker applied.

Notation
  MODE      BYTE   low 8 bits read as unsigned, silence = 128. The classic.
            WORD   low 16 bits read as signed. Wider, smoother, for drones.
  post      drive/tone/crush = saturation / lowpass / sample-and-hold
            spc = feedback delay: time / feedback / wet mix
  %FS       measured RMS as a fraction of full scale
  zc        zero crossings per 2000 samples -- a rough pitch indicator.
            zc 2-4 is subsonic rumble. zc 500+ is bright and noisy.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLASSIC BYTEBEAT -- memoryless f(t), bright and fast
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. sierpinski                                    BYTE   65% FS   zc 34
   t*(t>>p0&t>>p1)
   p0=12 p1=8   post: none

   A ramp gated by two shifted copies of itself. Because the shifts are
   powers of two, every gating term is exactly an octave from every other,
   so the pattern nests inside itself at every timescale -- the waveform,
   the note, and the phrase are the same shape. Sweep p0 and p1 against
   each other; they cannot go out of tune.

2. crowd                                         BYTE   59% FS   zc 276
   t*((t>>p0|t>>p1)&p2&t>>4)
   p0=12 p1=8 p2=63   post: none

   Viznut's original, the expression that started all of this. The OR of
   two shifts picks which octaves sound together; p2 masks the carrier and
   is effectively a timbre knob. Try p2 at 63, 127, 3.

3. minimal                                       BYTE   33% FS   zc 42
   t&t>>p0
   p0=8   post: none

   The entire idea in five characters. A fast ramp ANDed with a ramp 2^p0
   times slower: the slow one acts as an envelope on the fast one. It is
   quiet and lopsided at low t and opens up as t grows, because t>>8 gains
   bits over time. Also the patch that motivated the DC blocker -- see
   NOTES.md section 2.

4. modulo pitch                                  BYTE   58% FS   zc 625
   (t%(p0+1))*(t>>p1)
   p0=63 p1=9   post: none

   Modulo resets the ramp every p0+1 samples, and p0+1 need not be a power
   of two. This is how you escape the octave lattice: p0 is literally a
   pitch knob, and sweeping it glides continuously instead of in octaves.
   The only patch here where you can play a tune.

5. ring metal                                    BYTE   56% FS   zc 824
   (t*p0&t>>p1)*(t*p2&t>>p3)
   p0=5 p1=7 p2=3 p3=10   post: drive 30, tone 220

   Two amplitude-modulated tones multiplied together, which produces sum
   and difference frequencies. Those are not harmonically related to
   either source, so the result is inharmonic and bell-like -- struck
   metal rather than a note. p0 against p2 is the interval.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
DEATH INDUSTRIAL -- stateful, low, filtered, slow
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

6. two saws                                      WORD   57% FS   zc 12
   lp(t*p0^(t*p1>>1),p2)
   p0=48 p1=49 p2=40   post: drive 40, tone 90, spc 140/150/60

   In WORD mode, t*n & 0xffff is a sawtooth at n*sr/65536 Hz -- so p0 is a
   sub-bass frequency control. Two of them one unit apart beat against each
   other slowly; the XOR makes the interference gritty rather than smooth.
   The lp() is what turns it from a buzz into a drone. Move p0 and p1
   further apart for faster beating.

7. rumble                                        WORD   34% FS   zc 3
   lp(s0=s0-(s0>>6)+((t>>p0&1)?p1:-p1),p2)
   p0=9 p1=200 p2=60   post: drive 60, tone 70, spc 90/120/40

   A square wave fed into a leaky integrator, which is a triangle wave --
   but built from state rather than from t, so it has weight. The -(s0>>6)
   term is the leak: without it the integrator drifts off to infinity on
   any asymmetry. p0 is pitch (period = 2^(p0+1) samples), p1 is amplitude,
   p2 is darkness. At p0=9 this sits around 43 Hz.

8. hangar                                        WORD   58% FS   zc 2
   w(lp((t*p0^t>>p1)+(d(p2*64+1)>>1),p3))
   p0=32 p1=11 p2=180 p3=48   post: drive 30, tone 80, spc 200/200/90

   The output is written back into the delay line and read a third of a
   second later at half level. That feedback loop is what makes a static
   drone feel like it is happening in a very large room. p2 is the delay
   time -- short values comb-filter into a resonant pitch, long ones smear.
   The >>1 sets loop gain; raise it toward >>0 and it will run away.

9. beat gate                                     WORD   19% FS   zc 90
   (bt<bl>>p0)*((r>>p1)^lp(t*p2,p3))
   p0=3 p1=18 p2=40 p3=60   post: drive 50, tone 110, spc 160/180/80

   bt counts within the beat and bl is the beat length, so bt < bl>>p0
   opens a window at the top of every beat and closes it after 1/2^p0 of
   the beat. A noise burst over a drone, on the grid. p0 is gate length --
   raise it for a tighter, more percussive hit. Set the tempo with TAB to
   'bpm'.

10. loop + drift                                 WORD   20% FS   zc 684
    lp(((k>>p0&k>>p1)*p2<<6)^(r>>p3),p4)
    p0=9 p1=12 p2=60 p3=18 p4=100
    post: drive 40, tone 100, crush 20, spc 180/170/70

    The one you asked for. k wraps at the loop length, so the first half of
    this expression repeats EXACTLY every loop, forever. r never repeats.
    Put both in one expression and you have a fixed figure with noise
    drifting over it that never lands the same way twice. p3 controls how
    much noise (lower = more). Press K to restart the loop.

11. tape wobble                                  WORD   58% FS   zc 4
    w(lp(t*p0,p1))+d(p2*32+(t>>p3&511))
    p0=36 p1=50 p2=200 p3=7   post: drive 30, tone 75, spc 220/190/110

    The delay tap itself is modulated by a slow ramp, so the read pointer
    moves through the buffer at a varying rate -- which is pitch shift.
    The result slips and wanders like a stretched tape loop. p3 sets the
    wobble rate, p2 the centre delay.

12. bar switch                                   WORD   57% FS   zc 2
    lp(t*((n&3)==3?p0:p1)^(t>>p2),p3)
    p0=27 p1=40 p2=10 p3=45   post: drive 50, tone 85, spc 170/160/70

    n is a monotonic bar counter, so (n&3)==3 is true on every fourth bar.
    The fundamental drops for one bar in four and then returns. This is how
    you get structure over minutes instead of milliseconds -- try (n&7),
    or nest two conditions.

13. collapse                                     WORD   54% FS   zc 4
    lp(((t>>p0)*(t>>p1)*(t>>p2))&0xffff,p3)
    p0=6 p1=8 p2=10 p3=35
    post: drive 90, tone 60, crush 30, spc 150/200/100

    Three shifted ramps multiplied together. The product overflows 16 bits
    constantly and the &0xffff wraps it, so the waveform tears itself apart
    on a schedule set by the shifts. Heavily filtered afterwards, which
    leaves the tearing as texture and the periodicity as pitch. The
    heaviest patch here; drive is at 90 for a reason.

14. static field                                 WORD   19% FS   zc 275
    (lp(r>>p0,p1)*((t>>p2&p3)+1))>>3
    p0=14 p1=20 p2=13 p3=7
    post: drive 20, tone 55, spc 240/210/120

    Filtered noise whose amplitude is modulated by a slow bitwise ramp: a
    bed that breathes. p1 is how dark the noise is, p3 how deep the
    breathing. Sits underneath anything else. Turn the master gain down and
    leave it running.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGERED NOISE RACK -- hits, not samples
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The six sources below are in the SOURCE row after the eleven continuous
bytebeat engines. Selecting one enables the sequencer and seeds a rhythm when
the layer is empty. `tr` is 1 for one sample at each hit, `age` resets to 0,
and `vel` is 148 for a normal hit or 256 for an accent. `bp(x,f,q)` is a
resonant body: f is its pitch coefficient and high q rings longer.

thump
  bp(tr*vel*4096,p0,p1)
  p0=2 p1=244

  An impulse striking a low resonator. p0 moves the body from sub-bass into
  a knock; p1 moves from dead impact toward a long mechanical ring.

burst
  hp(lp(r>>p0,p1),p2)
  p0=18 p1=88 p2=8

  A filtered white-noise strike. The layer gate supplies its amplitude
  envelope; p0 is grain, p1 the upper edge, p2 the lower edge. Hat, snare,
  steam valve and torn speaker are neighboring settings.

metal
  bp(((((age*p0)^(age*p1))&65535)-32768),p2,p3)
  p0=23 p1=31 p2=24 p3=235

  Two bitwise partials restart in the same phase on every hit and excite a
  resonator. p0/p1 set the interval, p2 the struck body's pitch, p3 its ring.

dust
  ((r&255)<p0?(r>>p1):0)
  p0=12 p1=18

  Random microscopic impacts inside the gate. p0 is density and p1 is grain
  size. Lower probability on the sequence lane makes the whole texture skip;
  p0 changes what happens inside each surviving hit.

rumble
  lp((r>>p0)+bp(tr*vel*512,p1,p2),p3)
  p0=21 p1=3 p2=242 p3=24

  Low moving noise wrapped around a struck body. It supplies weight beneath a
  beat without becoming a clean kick.

feedback
  bp((tr*vel*512)+(r>>p0),p1,p2)
  p0=24 p1=10 p2=250

  A near-unstable resonator continually fed with noise and hit impulses. High
  p2 keeps its history; SPACE FREEZE can catch the larger phrase around it.

The new sequence lanes are part of these patches. Ratchets generate multiple
sample-accurate `tr` impulses in one step; probability is decided once before
the ratchet; parameter locks can change any p knob or post control per step.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
THINGS TO TRY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Turn ONE knob slowly. That is the instrument. A shift amount swept by hand
across four or five values does more than any expression you can write.

Sweep TONE down to 1 and back. Everything here becomes a different piece.

Replace t with k in any of the classic patches (1-5) and they become loops.

Wrap anything in w(...) and add + (d(20000)>>1) to give it a room.

Take a bright patch, set crush high and tone low. That is the whole death
industrial recipe in two knobs.

Set bpm to 40 and bars to 8 and let a loop patch run for ten minutes.

Use two registers as a counter and a comparator to build a gate:
  s0 = s0+1, then (s0%(p0*100+1) < p1*10) * <your patch>

To hear real low-sample-rate aliasing rather than ALSA's interpolation of
it, start with -R and press { a few times.

Headless check of a patch before you commit to it:
  ./bytebeat -E 'your expression' -p 12,8,63 -n 200
